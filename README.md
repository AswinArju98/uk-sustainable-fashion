## Project Overview

This project analyses consumer interest in sustainable fashion in the
United Kingdom using Google Trends data covering the period from 2015 to
2025.

The study examines changes in relative search interest for the term
“sustainable fashion” and considers what these changes may indicate
about consumer information-seeking behaviour and their implications for
fashion marketing.

Google Trends is used as a digital marketing intelligence tool to
identify changes in consumer attention and search behaviour. The
findings are interpreted cautiously because relative search interest
does not represent actual purchases, sales, or overall market demand.

## Research Aim

To examine consumer interest in sustainable fashion in the UK through
Google Trends data.

## Research Question

**What insights can be gained from Google Trends data regarding consumer
interest in sustainable fashion in the UK?**

## Research Objectives

The project aims to:

- Analyse interest in sustainable-fashion-related search activity over
  time.
- Identify trends and significant changes in search activity.
- Examine regional variation in UK search interest.
- Identify related search terms associated with sustainable fashion.
- Develop recommendations for using search-interest data in a
  sustainable fashion business’s digital marketing strategy.

## Data Source

The study uses secondary quantitative data obtained from Google Trends.

### Google Trends Data Parameters

| Parameter       | Selection                       |
|-----------------|---------------------------------|
| Search term     | Sustainable Fashion             |
| Geography       | United Kingdom                  |
| Period          | 2015–2025                       |
| Category        | All categories                  |
| Google property | Web Search                      |
| Measurement     | Relative search interest, 0–100 |

Google Trends data are sampled, aggregated, anonymised and normalised

## Research Methodology

The study follows a quantitative, descriptive and longitudinal approach
based on secondary data.

The analysis examines changes in relative search interest rather than
attempting to establish causal relationships.

The Google Trends data were exported as a CSV file and processed for
analysis. The raw data and processed data are kept separately to support
data integrity and reproducibility.

## Analytical Periods

The 2015–2025 period was divided into three analytical periods for
comparison.

| Analytical Period | Purpose                         |
|-------------------|---------------------------------|
| 2015–2017         | Early-stage interest            |
| 2018–2020         | Major development               |
| 2021–2025         | Established/high-level interest |

These classifications are analytical categories used to compare changes
over time and are not intended to establish that a particular event
caused the changes.

## Key Findings

The analysis identified a substantial increase in relative Google Trends
search interest across the three analytical periods.

### Mean Search Interest

| Analytical Period | Mean Search Interest |
|-------------------|---------------------:|
| 2015–2017         |                16.97 |
| 2018–2020         |                54.92 |
| 2021–2025         |                61.82 |

### Percentage Change

| Period Comparison      | Percentage Change |
|------------------------|------------------:|
| 2015–2017 to 2018–2020 |          +223.57% |
| 2018–2020 to 2021–2025 |           +12.56% |
| 2015–2017 to 2021–2025 |          +264.22% |

The largest increase occurred between 2015–2017 and 2018–2020, followed
by continued growth at a more moderate rate during 2021–2025.

The findings indicate that sustainable fashion became considerably more
prominent in UK online search activity during the study period.

However, the increase in search interest should not be interpreted as an
equivalent increase in sustainable-fashion purchases or sales. Search
activity may reflect consumer awareness, information seeking, curiosity,
product evaluation, or comparison between alternatives.

## Sustainable Fashion as an Emerging Consumer Agenda

The research identifies several sustainable-fashion-related search
terms, including:

- Sustainable clothing
- Sustainable clothing brands
- Sustainable clothing brands UK
- Sustainable swimwear
- Sustainable jeans
- Affordable sustainable clothing

These searches may represent different information needs.

General searches may indicate early information gathering, while
searches for sustainable clothing brands may indicate comparison between
alternatives. Product-specific searches may indicate product evaluation,
while affordability-related searches introduce cost as an explicit
consideration.

This supports the use of search behaviour to understand evolving
consumer information needs and to develop more focused digital content
strategies.

## Search Behaviour and Consumer Perspectives

The findings demonstrate the potential usefulness of Google Trends for
understanding online consumer interest and information search behaviour.

However, increased search activity should not be treated as evidence of
increased sustainable-fashion purchases.

The report identifies an attitude–behaviour gap in sustainable fashion
consumption. Consumers may express positive sustainability attitudes
while facing barriers that prevent these attitudes from becoming
purchasing behaviour.

### Selected Consumer Findings

| Measure                                      | Reported Result |
|----------------------------------------------|----------------:|
| Sustainability important to wardrobe choices |             60% |
| Cost identified as a barrier                 |             61% |
| Lack of information identified as a barrier  |             50% |

These findings should be interpreted as contextual evidence from the
cited studies and not as components of a single dataset.

Google Trends is therefore treated as an indicator of consumer interest
and information needs rather than a direct measure of market demand.

## Marketing Implications

The findings provide several implications for UK fashion marketers.

### Content Strategy

Businesses can develop content addressing topics consumers actively
search for, including sustainable materials, ethical production,
clothing durability, repair, recycling, and responsible sourcing.

### Search Engine Optimisation

Search trends and related search terminology can help marketers
understand the language consumers use when researching sustainable
fashion. This can support more targeted SEO and content strategies.

### Product Positioning

Sustainability can support product positioning, but environmental claims
should be specific, transparent, and evidence-based to reduce
greenwashing concerns.

### Campaign Planning

Businesses can monitor changes in search interest when planning
sustainability campaigns and identifying emerging consumer information
needs.

### Affordability and Value

Searches relating to affordability indicate that sustainable fashion
should not necessarily be positioned only as a premium proposition. Cost
remains an important consideration in sustainable consumption.

### Commercial Validation

Google Trends should not be used independently to estimate market
demand. Search-interest data can be combined with:

- Website analytics
- Sales data
- Conversion rates
- Customer research
- Market reports

This helps distinguish online attention from commercially validated
demand.

## Theoretical Framework

The project draws on three key perspectives.

### Consumer Information Search Behaviour Theory

This perspective is used to understand how consumers search for
information, reduce uncertainty, and compare alternatives before
purchasing.

### Theory of Planned Behaviour

The Theory of Planned Behaviour is used as a supplementary framework for
understanding the relationship between attitudes, intentions, perceived
behavioural control, and behaviour.

### Attitude–Behaviour Gap

The attitude–behaviour gap is used to explain why positive
sustainability attitudes and search interest do not necessarily
translate into sustainable purchasing behaviour.

## Limitations

### Google Trends Is Not a Sales Measure

Google Trends provides relative search interest rather than actual
purchases or sales. Search interest should therefore not be interpreted
as a direct measure of market demand.

### Single Primary Search Term

The main analysis focuses on the specific keyword “sustainable fashion”.
Consumers may use alternative terms such as ethical fashion, slow
fashion, eco-friendly clothing, second-hand fashion, and circular
fashion.

### Time-Averaged Analysis

The current analysis uses analytical periods rather than the original
monthly observations. Therefore, it cannot identify individual monthly
peaks, seasonal patterns, or temporary search spikes.

### No Causal Inference

The analysis identifies changes and patterns in search interest but does
not establish why those changes occurred or demonstrate that a
particular event caused them.

### Regional Analysis

The study focuses on the United Kingdom. Differences between individual
UK regions are not treated as a primary analytical focus.

## Future Research
