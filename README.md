# UK Sustainable Fashion Consumer Interest Analysis

**Author:** Aswin Kattil Parambath\
**Qualification:** MSc International Business and Management\
**Date:** August 2026

## Project Overview

This project examines consumer interest in sustainable fashion in the
United Kingdom using Google Trends data covering the period from 2015 to
2025.

The study analyses changes in relative search interest for the search
term "sustainable fashion" and considers what these changes may indicate
about consumer information-seeking behaviour and their implications for
fashion marketing.

The project uses Google Trends as a digital marketing intelligence tool
to identify changes in consumer attention, search behaviour and
potential information needs.

> **Important:** Google Trends measures relative search interest on a
> 0--100 scale. It does not measure actual purchases, sales or absolute
> search volume.

## Research Aim

To examine consumer interest in sustainable fashion in the UK through
Google Trends data.

## Research Question

**What insights can be gained from Google Trends data regarding consumer
interest in sustainable fashion in the UK?**

## Research Objectives

-   Analyse interest in sustainable-fashion-related search activity over
    time.
-   Identify trends and significant changes in search activity.
-   Examine regional variation in UK search interest.
-   Identify related search terms associated with sustainable fashion.
-   Develop recommendations for using search-interest data in a
    sustainable fashion business's digital marketing strategy.

## Data Source

The study uses secondary quantitative data obtained from Google Trends.

### Google Trends Parameters

  Parameter         Selection
  ----------------- ----------------------------------
  Search term       Sustainable Fashion
  Geography         United Kingdom
  Period            2015--2025
  Category          All categories
  Google property   Web Search
  Measurement       Relative search interest, 0--100

Google Trends data are sampled, aggregated, anonymised and normalised
according to the selected location and time period.

## Research Methodology

The study follows a:

-   Quantitative research approach
-   Descriptive research design
-   Longitudinal analysis
-   Secondary-data methodology

The analysis examines changes in relative search interest rather than
attempting to establish causal relationships.

The Google Trends data were exported as a CSV file and processed for
analysis. The analysis focuses on descriptive statistics and comparison
of search interest across defined analytical periods.

## Analytical Periods

The 2015--2025 period was divided into three analytical periods:

  Analytical Period   Purpose
  ------------------- ---------------------------------
  2015--2017          Early-stage interest
  2018--2020          Major development
  2021--2025          Established/high-level interest

These classifications are analytical categories used to compare changes
over time and are not intended to establish that a particular event
caused the changes.

## Key Findings

The analysis identified a substantial increase in relative Google Trends
search interest across the three analytical periods.

  Analytical Period     Mean Search Interest
  ------------------- ----------------------
  2015--2017                           16.97
  2018--2020                           54.92
  2021--2025                           61.82

### Period-to-Period Growth

-   **2015--2017 to 2018--2020:** +223.57%
-   **2018--2020 to 2021--2025:** +12.56%
-   **2015--2017 to 2021--2025:** +264.22%

The largest increase occurred between 2015--2017 and 2018--2020,
followed by continued growth at a more moderate rate during 2021--2025.

The findings indicate that sustainable fashion became considerably more
prominent in UK online search activity during the study period.

However, the increase in search interest should not be interpreted as an
equivalent increase in sustainable-fashion purchases or sales. Search
activity may reflect consumer awareness, information seeking, curiosity,
product evaluation or comparison between alternatives.

## Sustainable Fashion as an Emerging Consumer Agenda

The study also considers the increasing specificity of sustainable
fashion-related searches.

Examples identified in the research include:

-   Sustainable clothing
-   Sustainable clothing brands
-   Sustainable clothing brands UK
-   Sustainable swimwear
-   Sustainable jeans
-   Affordable sustainable clothing

These searches may represent different information needs, ranging from
general awareness to product evaluation, brand comparison and
affordability considerations.

The findings are considered in relation to Consumer Information Search
Behaviour Theory, which explains how consumers seek information, reduce
uncertainty and compare alternatives.

For marketers, this provides an opportunity to develop more targeted
content around sustainability, materials, ethical production, products,
brands and affordability.

## Search Behaviour and Consumer Perspectives

A key finding of the research is that consumer interest should not be
treated as equivalent to purchasing behaviour.

The study identifies an attitude--behaviour gap in sustainable fashion
consumption. Consumers may show positive attitudes towards
sustainability and actively search for sustainable fashion information
while still facing barriers that prevent those intentions from becoming
purchasing actions.

Selected findings discussed in the report include:

  Measure                      Reported Result
  -------------------------- -----------------
  Sustainability important                 60%
  Cost barrier                             61%
  Information barrier                      50%

These findings provide context for interpreting Google Trends data.

Potential barriers include:

-   Cost
-   Availability
-   Information
-   Convenience

Therefore, Google Trends is treated as an indicator of consumer interest
and information seeking rather than a direct measure of market demand.

## Marketing Implications

The findings provide several implications for UK fashion marketers.

### Content Strategy

Businesses can use search behaviour to identify topics consumers want
information about, including sustainable materials, ethical production,
product durability, repair, recycling and responsible sourcing.

### Search Engine Optimisation

Related search terminology can help marketers understand the language
and questions consumers use when researching sustainable fashion. This
can support the development of more relevant SEO and digital content
strategies.

### Product Positioning

Sustainability can be incorporated into product positioning, but
environmental claims should be specific, clear and evidence-based to
reduce the risk of greenwashing.

### Campaign Planning

Changes in search interest can be monitored when planning sustainability
campaigns and developing content around emerging consumer information
needs.

### Affordability

Searches relating to affordability suggest that sustainable fashion
should not automatically be positioned as a premium or luxury
proposition. Cost remains an important consideration in sustainable
consumption.

### Commercial Validation

Google Trends should be combined with other business evidence,
including:

-   Website analytics
-   Sales data
-   Conversion rates
-   Customer research
-   Market reports

This helps distinguish online attention from validated commercial
demand.

## Theoretical Framework

The project draws on three key perspectives.

### Consumer Information Search Behaviour Theory

This perspective is used to understand how consumers search for
information, compare alternatives and reduce uncertainty before
purchasing.

### Theory of Planned Behaviour

The Theory of Planned Behaviour is used as a supplementary framework for
understanding relationships between attitudes, intentions and
behavioural control.

### Attitude--Behaviour Gap

The attitude--behaviour gap is used to explain why positive
sustainability attitudes and search interest do not necessarily
translate into sustainable purchasing behaviour.

## Limitations

### Google Trends Is Not a Sales Measure

Google Trends provides relative search interest rather than actual
purchases or sales. Search interest should therefore not be interpreted
as direct evidence of market demand.

### Single Primary Search Term

The main analysis focuses on the specific keyword "sustainable fashion".
Consumers may use alternative terms such as ethical fashion, slow
fashion, eco-friendly clothing, second-hand fashion or circular fashion.

### Time-Averaged Analysis

The main analysis uses analytical periods and period averages. This
limits the ability to identify individual monthly or seasonal peaks and
temporary search spikes.

### No Causal Inference

The analysis identifies changes and patterns in search interest but does
not establish why those changes occurred or demonstrate that a
particular event caused them.

### Search Behaviour and Purchase Behaviour

An increase in online search activity does not necessarily result in a
purchase. Factors such as affordability, availability and information
can influence whether consumer intentions become purchasing behaviour.
