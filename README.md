Project Overview
This project analyses consumer interest in sustainable fashion in the United Kingdom using Google Trends data covering the period from 2015 to 2025. The study examines changes in relative search interest for the term "sustainable fashion" and considers what these changes may indicate about consumer information-seeking behaviour and their implications for fashion marketing.
The project uses Google Trends as a low-cost digital marketing intelligence tool to identify changes in consumer attention, search behaviour and potential information needs.
Important: Google Trends measures relative search interest on a 0–100 scale. It does not measure actual purchases, sales or the number of individual consumers.
Research Aim
To examine consumer interest in sustainable fashion in the UK through Google Trends data.
Research Question
What insights can be gained from Google Trends data regarding consumer interest in sustainable fashion in the UK?
Research Objectives
•	Analyse interest in sustainable-fashion-related search activity over time.
•	Identify trends and significant changes in search activity.
•	Examine regional variation in UK search interest.
•	Identify related search terms associated with sustainable fashion.
•	Develop recommendations for using search-interest data in a sustainable fashion business's digital marketing strategy.

Research Methodology
The study follows a:
•	Quantitative research approach
•	Descriptive research design
•	Longitudinal analysis
•	Secondary-data methodology
The analysis examines changes in relative search interest rather than attempting to establish causal relationships.
The original Google Trends data were exported as a CSV file. The raw data were maintained separately from the processed data to support data integrity and reproducibility.
Analytical Periods
Analytical Period	Purpose
2015–2017	Early-stage interest
2018–2020	Major development
2021–2025	Established/high-level interest

These classifications are analytical categories used to compare changes over time and are not intended to establish that a particular event caused the changes.
Key Findings
The analysis identified a substantial increase in relative Google Trends search interest across the three analytical periods.
Analytical Period	Mean Search Interest
2015–2017	16.97
2018–2020	54.92
2021–2025	61.82

Period-to-period growth
•	2015–2017 → 2018–2020: +223.57%
•	2018–2020 → 2021–2025: +12.56%
•	2015–2017 → 2021–2025: +264.22%
The largest increase occurred between 2015–2017 and 2018–2020, followed by continued growth at a more moderate rate during 2021–2025.
Key interpretation
The findings indicate that sustainable fashion became considerably more prominent in UK online search activity during the study period.
However, the increase in search interest should not be interpreted as an equivalent increase in sustainable-fashion purchases or sales.
•	Consumer awareness
•	Information seeking
•	Curiosity
•	Product evaluation
•	Comparison between alternatives
Consumer Search Behaviour
The study also considers the increasing specificity of sustainable fashion-related searches.
•	Sustainable clothing
•	Sustainable clothing brands
•	Sustainable clothing brands UK
•	Sustainable swimwear
•	Sustainable jeans
•	Affordable sustainable clothing
These searches may represent different information needs, ranging from general awareness to product evaluation, brand comparison and affordability considerations.
This supports the application of Consumer Information Search Behaviour Theory, which considers online search as part of the process through which consumers obtain information, reduce uncertainty and compare alternatives.
Consumer Interest–Purchase Behaviour Gap
A key finding of the research is that consumer interest should not be treated as equivalent to purchasing behaviour.
The project identifies an attitude–behaviour gap in sustainable fashion consumption.
Measure	Reported Result
Sustainability importance	60%
Cost barrier	61%
Information barrier	50%

These findings provide context for interpreting Google Trends data. Consumers may show strong sustainability interest while still facing barriers such as cost, availability, information and convenience.
Therefore, Google Trends is treated as an indicator of consumer interest and information seeking, rather than a direct measure of market demand.
Marketing Implications
1. Content Strategy
Search behaviour can help identify topics consumers want information about, including sustainable materials, ethical production, product durability, repair, recycling and responsible sourcing.
2. SEO
Related search terminology can help marketers understand the language and questions consumers use when researching sustainable fashion.
3. Product Positioning
Sustainability can be incorporated into product positioning, but environmental claims should be specific, clear and evidence-based. This can help reduce the risk of greenwashing.
4. Campaign Planning
Changes in search interest can be monitored when planning sustainability campaigns and developing new content.
5. Affordability
Searches relating to affordability indicate that sustainable fashion should not automatically be positioned as a premium or luxury proposition.
6. Commercial Validation
Google Trends should be combined with website analytics, sales data, conversion rates, customer research and market reports to distinguish online attention from validated commercial demand.

Limitations
Google Trends is not a sales measure
Google Trends provides relative search interest rather than actual purchases or sales.
Single primary search term
The main analysis focuses on the specific keyword "sustainable fashion". Consumers may use alternative terms such as ethical fashion, slow fashion, eco-friendly clothing, second-hand fashion and circular fashion.
Time-averaged analysis
The current analysis uses analytical periods rather than the original monthly observations. Therefore, it cannot identify individual monthly peaks, seasonal patterns or temporary search spikes.
No causal inference
The analysis identifies changes and patterns in search interest but does not establish why those changes occurred.

Theoretical Framework
Consumer Information Search Behaviour Theory
Used to understand how consumers search for information, compare alternatives and reduce uncertainty before purchasing.
Theory of Planned Behaviour
Used as a supplementary framework for understanding the relationship between attitudes, intentions and behavioural control.
Attitude–Behaviour Gap
Used to explain why positive sustainability attitudes and search interest do not necessarily translate into sustainable purchasing behaviour.
